What 0 to 100 means

This is the most important page in the handbook. The six buttons a coach taps in the app mean the same thing school-wide — otherwise coach A's 60% and coach B's 60% are not the same thing, and the percentage a parent sees means nothing.

0
Not taught, not assessedThe skill was not worked on this lesson. Leave it where it is.
20
Shown it, cannot do itThey understand what is being asked. The body cannot do it yet.
40
Can do it with heavy helpYour hands are still on them, or they are on a float the whole way.
60
Can do it with light help or a cueAn occasional hand, or a spoken reminder to get it right.
80
Unaided, but not consistent or not to standardManaged it today, may not tomorrow; or did it right but short of the distance or time.
100
Unaided, consistent, and meets the pass standardNo cue needed, did it last lesson too, and meets what the pass standard says.

How the percentage is worked out. A stage's percentage is the average of its skills. So taking one skill from 0 to 100 moves the parent's progress bar exactly as far as nudging five skills up by 20 — but the first means something was actually learnt, and the second means everything was touched once. Take one skill to 100 rather than leaving five at 40.

A stage is complete only when every skill in it reads 100, and all three stages must be complete before a level-up. The level-up is pressed by an administrator; it does not happen on its own.

Every lesson

How to teach it, how to record it

  • Only move the skills you actually worked on today. Leave the rest where they are. Nudging a whole stage up one notch because it feels about right is the fastest way to turn the record into waste paper.
  • One notch at a time. Jumping 40 straight to 100 means the two lessons in between were never written down, and the parent cannot see how their child got there.
  • 100 takes two lessons. Unprompted today and unprompted last lesson. Once is 80.
  • Two or three skills per lesson. In thirty minutes, working five skills means none of them were worked.
  • Teach the stages in order. The app will not let you fill in stage 2 before stage 1 is finished. That is deliberate, not broken.
  • Go backwards when they are stuck. Stuck on the whip kick? Go back to driving the dolphin kick from the hip. Stuck on breathing? Go back to blowing bubbles. Dropping back a step is not losing ground; pushing on regardless is.
  • The pass standard is beside the button. When you are unsure what to give, read that line rather than going on impression.

Breath-holding: three rules that are never bent. The Red Cross writes without hyperventilating after its underwater swimming item, because holding the breath after hyperventilating causes shallow-water blackout, and a person loses consciousness without struggling — usually with nobody noticing. So: one, never allow repeated deep breaths before going under. Two, never run a breath-holding contest of any kind, including between swimmers. Three, if anyone reports dizziness, tingling or feeling unwell, the underwater work ends for that lesson. This curriculum has three underwater items (L4 underwater kicking, L5 breath holding 15 sec, L6 underwater dolphin kick) and all three are covered by this.

Seven levels

From never in the water to four race-legal strokes

Lesson counts are estimates, not promises. Within the same level a child swimming twice a week gets through it more than twice as fast as one swimming once — the gap is shorter, so less is forgotten. Give parents a range, never a single number.

Level 1

Water Discovery

A child who has never been in water, from "there are rules near the pool" to "my face goes in and my body floats". This level teaches safety and trust, not swimming.

Stage 13 skills

Safe Entry and Recognition

Pass standard
Walks to the edge, waits for the coach, enters feet-first without being asked twice.
How to teach it
The first lesson is not about swimming, it is about there being rules at the water's edge. Every time: walk to the spot, look at you, wait for the word, then go in.
Where they get stuck
An adult in a hurry lifts the child straight in. Skip the waiting once and you rebuild it every lesson afterwards.

Safe Exit

Pass standard
Climbs out unaided using the ladder or the wall, on their own.
How to teach it
Getting out matters as much as getting in — it is the only way back if they fall. Both hands gripped before they push.
Where they get stuck
The coach reflexively hauls them out, and the child never learns to climb out at all.

Wall Grasping

Pass standard
Holds the wall unaided and moves along it 5 yd hand-over-hand.
How to teach it
Fingers hooked over the lip of the wall, not a flat palm pressing. Moving along, the new hand grips before the old one lets go.
Where they get stuck
Both hands off at once to change grip. Ask for one hand on the wall at all times.
Stage 23 skills

Water Walking

Pass standard
Walks 10 yd in chest-deep water, upright, without holding anything.
How to teach it
Let them walk themselves. When they get nervous your hand goes on their back, not in their hand. Stretch the distance gradually.
Where they get stuck
Walking ten yards hand in hand — that is you walking, not them.

Underwater Bubble Blowing

Pass standard
Face in the water, blows bubbles from the mouth for 5 seconds without lifting the head.
How to teach it
Blow at the surface from the deck first, then mouth in, then nose. Out through the mouth, never in through the nose.
Where they get stuck
Holding the breath, dipping the face and lifting it straight back out. That is breath holding, not blowing out, and breathing will choke them later.

Flutter Kick on Deck

Pass standard
Sitting on the edge, kicks from the hip with straight legs and pointed toes for 20 seconds.
How to teach it
Sitting on the edge so they can watch their own legs. Legs like sticks, toes pressed down.
Where they get stuck
Bent knees, pedalling a bicycle. A light hand on the knee shows them the drive comes from the hip, not the knee.
Stage 33 skills

Assisted Floating

Pass standard
Floats on the back for 5 seconds with the coach supporting one hand only.
How to teach it
Support under the shoulder blades, not under the head. Take the support away a little at a time so they discover the water holds them up.
Where they get stuck
Holding the head. Once the head is held they never learn to relax back.

Float-to-Stand Transition

Pass standard
From a front or back float, gets both feet under the body and stands up unaided.
How to teach it
This is a safety skill and matters more than the float itself. Knees to the chest, feet down, head up last.
Where they get stuck
Head first, then standing — lift the head and the body sinks, they swallow water and start fearing the float.

Superman Glide

Pass standard
Pushes off the wall and glides face-down 5 yd, arms extended, no kicking.
How to teach it
Arms squeezing the ears, chin tucked, feet together. Nothing happens during the glide: let them feel the water carrying them.
Where they get stuck
Lifting the head to look ahead. The head comes up, the hips drop, and the glide stops.
Level 2

Water Confidence

Floating comes first, then moving. Kicking, rolling, the first crossings of deep water. The word for this level is relax — the harder they try, the more they sink.

Stage 13 skills

Freestyle Kick 10 yd (Board)

Pass standard
Kicks 10 yd on a board, face in the water, legs long and from the hip.
How to teach it
Hold the front edge of the board, arms straight, face in. Small fast kick with the splash staying under the surface.
Where they get stuck
Big slow kicks with bent knees. Ask for heels that barely break the surface.

Backstroke Kick 10 yd

Pass standard
Kicks 10 yd on the back, ears in the water, hips at the surface.
How to teach it
Ears in the water, stomach pushed up. Eyes on one fixed point on the ceiling.
Where they get stuck
Kicking while sitting — hips sunk. A hand under the hips, lifting once, shows them where they should be.

Push-Off Float

Pass standard
Pushes off the wall into a front float and holds it 5 seconds before standing.
How to teach it
Joins the push-off to the float. Every start and turn later is built on this.
Where they get stuck
Struggling the moment they leave the wall. Ask for nothing at all until they have counted to five.
Stage 23 skills

BBQ Roll (Body Rotation)

Pass standard
While kicking, rolls from front to side to back and back again without stopping.
How to teach it
BBQ as in meat turning on a spit. The kick never stops; only the body rotates.
Where they get stuck
The legs stop during the roll. Keep saying: the legs are not allowed to stop.

Bubble Jumps (Chest Deep)

Pass standard
Ten jumps in chest-deep water: submerge, blow out, surface, repeat rhythmically.
How to teach it
Build the rhythm: out on the way down, in on the way up. Ten without a break matters more than ten that look pretty.
Where they get stuck
Breathing in underwater. Practise the phrase in shallow water first: down and out, up and in.

Breath Holding 5 sec

Pass standard
Fully submerged, eyes open, holds 5 seconds and surfaces calmly.
How to teach it
Eyes open is the point. Breath holding with the eyes shut is worthless, because in a real fall they need to see.
Where they get stuck
Pinching the nose with the fingers. They need to learn to keep water out with a gentle breath out through the nose.
Stage 34 skills

Object Retrieval

Pass standard
Retrieves an object from the bottom in chest-deep water, first try.
How to teach it
Their first time looking for something underwater with their eyes open. Put it where they can see it; do not make it hard.
Where they get stuck
Repeating a failed dive again and again costs them confidence. Put it shallower instead.

Starfish Float 10 sec

Pass standard
Back float, arms and legs spread, 10 seconds unaided and unmoving.
How to teach it
Relaxing is the whole technique. Tell them: the harder you try, the more you sink. Arms and legs spread like a starfish.
Where they get stuck
Arching the back with effort. A light press on the stomach lets them relax.

Bubble Jumps (Over Head)

Pass standard
Same as chest-deep but in water over their head, ten times, holding the wall.
How to teach it
Over head-deep, done holding the wall — the wall is where the courage comes from. This is the bridge into deep water.
Where they get stuck
Straight into the middle of the deep end. They grab you, and the fear lasts longer.

Sculling 5 yd

Pass standard
Sculls the hands figure-of-eight and travels 5 yd on the back without kicking.
How to teach it
Sculling is the first lesson in feeling the water; every catch in every stroke comes from here. Figure of eight, palms spreading jam.
Where they get stuck
Slapping up and down instead of sculling. Slow it down until they find the palm pressing on the water.
Level 3

Independent Movement

The first time they move through the water on their own. Breathing, treading and fall-in survival come in here. By the end of this level a child who falls into deep water can get themselves back to the wall.

Stage 13 skills

Freestyle Kick 25 yd

Pass standard
Kicks 25 yd on a board with side breathing, no standing up.
How to teach it
Breathing is added here. The head turns to the side rather than lifting, with one goggle staying in the water.
Where they get stuck
Lifting the head to breathe. Use the cue: turn with your ear on your arm.

Backstroke Kick 25 yd

Pass standard
Kicks 25 yd on the back, arms at the sides, staying in a straight line.
How to teach it
Twenty-five yards has to hold a straight line. Teach them to use the lane line or a fixed point on the ceiling.
Where they get stuck
Swimming a snake. Every five yards, remind them to find the point on the ceiling again.

Streamline Push-Off

Pass standard
Push-off in a tight streamline and glides 7 yd before the first kick.
How to teach it
Hands stacked and squeezing the ears, body like a stick. The distance of the glide is the only proof the streamline is any good.
Where they get stuck
Arms not squeezed, or feet planted too low on the wall.
Stage 23 skills

Treading Water 30 sec

Pass standard
Treads water 30 seconds in deep water, chin clear, without touching the wall.
How to teach it
Teach the eggbeater first — circles — then add the hands. Treading is the core of deep-water safety; do not rush it.
Where they get stuck
Hands slapping frantically while the body bobs straight up and down. Ask for slow, big legs.

Deep Water Object Retrieval

Pass standard
Retrieves an object from the bottom of deep water and returns to the wall.
How to teach it
Deep-water retrieval pulls together the breath holding, the open eyes and the dive. Go down with them the first time.
Where they get stuck
Starting at the deepest point. Start at the shallow end of the deep water.

Survival Float

Pass standard
Survival float face-down for 1 minute, lifting the head only to breathe.
How to teach it
The survival float is about saving energy, not about looking good. The face goes back in the water after each breath.
Where they get stuck
Keeping the head up the whole time. That will not last a minute.
Stage 34 skills

Roll-and-Breathe Swim 15 yd

Pass standard
Swims 15 yd rolling front-to-side to breathe, without stopping.
How to teach it
This is freestyle before freestyle. Rolling to breathe comes first; the arms come after.
Where they get stuck
Teaching the arm pull straight away. Freestyle without the roll is very hard to fix later.

In-Water Turn

Pass standard
Swims to the wall, turns without standing, and pushes off the other way.
How to teach it
Turning without standing up is the threshold of swimming continuously. The feet find the wall before they push.
Where they get stuck
Standing up at the turn to catch a breath. Ask for the face to stay near the water throughout.

Dolphin Kick 10 yd

Pass standard
Dolphin kicks 10 yd on the front, the wave starting at the chest not the knees.
How to teach it
The dolphin kick sits here as body-wave training, not to teach butterfly. The wave starts at the chest.
Where they get stuck
Kicking from a bent knee. A hand on their back lets them feel the wave travelling down.

Water Safety Test

Pass standard
Jump in, turn around, swim 5 yd back to the wall and climb out unaided.
How to teach it
This is the Level 3 test and sits last on purpose — every skill before it exists for this. Jump, turn, swim, climb out, in one go.
Where they get stuck
Beautiful in pieces, a mess joined up. Three clean run-throughs, with no prompting, before you pass it.
Level 4

Stroke Foundations

Real strokes start here. Freestyle and backstroke each take shape, an energy-saving stroke is added, and the level finishes against the Red Cross water-competency benchmark. By the end of it a child who falls into deep water can get themselves out.

About 20–24 thirty-minute lessons for the level
StageLessonsWhat this stage is for
Stage 16–7Freestyle 15 → 25 yd
Stage 27–8Backstroke, elementary backstroke, streamlined kicking
Stage 37–9Deep-water survival, finishing with the 25 yd competency test
Stage 13 skills

Freestyle 15 yd

Pass standard
Swims 15 yd freestyle, side breathing, arms recovering over the water.
How to teach it
Their first whole freestyle. High-elbow recovery, hand entering ahead of the shoulder line. Keep the distance short at first.
Where they get stuck
Straight arms swinging in a wide circle. Use the cue: elbow out of the water first.

Freestyle 25 yd

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd freestyle without stopping, breathing to one side every 2-3 strokes.
How to teach it
Twenty-five yards is the first real milestone. One fixed breathing side is enough; do not ask for both yet.
Where they get stuck
Thrashing to get to the end. Slower is fine — the stroke has to still be there.

Seated and Kneeling Dive

Pass standard
Enters head-first from sitting and then from kneeling, hands leading, without belly-flopping.
How to teach it
The angle of entry matters more than the posture. Get the seated dive solid before kneeling. Arms squeezing the ears, head hidden between them.
Where they get stuck
Lifting the head to look at the surface. Up comes the head and it becomes a belly flop — it hurts, and then they are afraid of it.
Stage 24 skills

Backstroke 15 yd

Pass standard
Swims 15 yd backstroke, hips up, arms straight past the ear.
How to teach it
The problem with backstroke is always sinking hips. Stomach pushed up, chin lightly tucked.
Where they get stuck
Kicking from bent knees, and arms that never go past the head.

Elementary Backstroke 25 yd

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd on the back with a symmetric arm pull and whip kick, gliding after each stroke.
How to teach it
Symmetric arms, whip kick, face up — this is the stroke they switch to when they are tired, and the long swims in L7 will need it. Chicken, aeroplane, rocket.
Where they get stuck
Arms and legs out of time. Practise the arms alone, then the legs alone, and only then put them together.

Streamline Freestyle Kicking

Pass standard
Kicks 25 yd in a tight streamline, both arms overhead, face down.
How to teach it
Joins the streamline to the kick. Everything that happens after a start is built on this.
Where they get stuck
Arms coming apart. Ask for hands stacked and squeezed.

Underwater Freestyle Kicking

Pass standard
Kicks 10 yd fully underwater in streamline on one breath.
How to teach it
Ten yards of kicking underwater on one breath, training the breath hold and the streamline at the same time.
Where they get stuck
Sneaking the head up halfway.
Stage 35 skills

Starfish Float 30 sec

Pass standard
Back float 30 seconds in deep water, relaxed, without sculling.
How to teach it
Floating in deep water is a nerve problem, not a technique problem. The thirty seconds is about relaxing.
Where they get stuck
The coach standing there talking the whole time. Go quiet and let them finish it themselves.

Treading Water 1 min

Pass standard
Treads water 1 minute in deep water with hands out of the water for the last 10 seconds.
How to teach it
Hands out of the water for the last ten seconds, to prove the legs are actually doing the work.
Where they get stuck
Sinking the moment the hands come up. Go back and build the legs before testing again.

No-Goggles Swim

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd freestyle with no goggles, eyes open, without panicking.
How to teach it
No goggles is what a real fall looks like. Get used to open eyes in shallow water before swimming it.
Where they get stuck
Swimming with the eyes screwed half shut. Ask for them properly open.

Clothed Swim (7 yd)

Pass standard
Swims 7 yd in a T-shirt and shorts, then removes the shirt in deep water.
How to teach it
Swimming clothed teaches them how heavy clothes are. Take the top off somewhere they can tread, not stand.
Where they get stuck
Taking it off where they can stand. Nothing is learnt that way.

Water Competency 25 yd

Pass standard
In deep water: jump in, surface and float or tread 1 min, turn a full circle to find the exit, swim 25 yd without stopping, climb out without the ladder.
How to teach it
The Red Cross water-competency benchmark, and the Level 4 test. Five actions in one go with no prompting in between. Once they pass it you can tell the parents they meet the national standard.
Where they get stuck
Beautiful in pieces, a mess joined up. Deep water, no goggles, three complete run-throughs before it counts.
Level 5

Stroke Development

Distance grows, turns appear, and the whip kick and dolphin kick start being laid down. Turns are taught open first, then flip. This is the longest level and the easiest to stall in — the whip kick is the hardest movement in the four strokes to teach.

StageLessonsWhat this stage is for
Stage 15–6Freestyle 50 → 100 yd
Stage 28–10Longer backstroke, open and flip turns, surface dive
Stage 38–10Whip kick and dolphin kick, rescue and breath holding
Stage 12 skills

Freestyle 50 yd

Pass standard
Swims 50 yd freestyle with one wall turn, stroke holding together throughout.
How to teach it
Fifty yards includes a turn, and the question is whether the stroke falls apart on the second length. Watch technique on the way back, not speed.
Where they get stuck
Sprinting the first length and falling apart on the second. Ask for the same stroke count both ways.

Freestyle 100 yd

Pass standard
Swims 100 yd freestyle continuous, bilateral breathing available if asked.
How to teach it
A hundred yards is where pacing starts to matter. Teach them to control it by stroke count, not by feel.
Where they get stuck
Breathing more often as the swim goes on. That is the signal the pace was too fast.
Stage 25 skills

Backstroke 25 yd

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd backstroke without stopping, shoulders rolling, head still.
How to teach it
Twenty-five yards of backstroke needs the shoulders rotating. Little finger enters the water first.
Where they get stuck
A flat body with no rotation, and arms thrown rather than swung.

Backstroke 50 yd

Pass standard
Swims 50 yd backstroke with one turn, staying in the lane.
How to teach it
With a turn added, the line still has to hold. Teach them to count strokes to know where the wall is.
Where they get stuck
Hitting the wall on backstroke. That is what happens without counting strokes, every time, so it has to be taught.

Open Turn

Pass standard
Two-hand touch, open turn and push-off for breaststroke and butterfly.
How to teach it
The open turn is used in all four strokes and is the fallback when a flip goes wrong, so it comes before the flip turn. Teach the touching hand first, then the turning body.
Where they get stuck
Standing up mid-turn to reset. Ask for feet that touch the wall and never the floor.

Freestyle Flip Turn

Pass standard
Approaches the wall, somersaults, plants both feet and pushes off on the back into streamline.
How to teach it
The somersault is not the point; the push-off is. Both feet planted together, and a streamline off the wall.
Where they get stuck
Looking for the wall after the flip. The distance has to be settled before they get there.

Surface Dive

Pass standard
Surface dives head-first to the bottom of deep water and returns with an object.
How to teach it
Head down, feet up: the dive is driven by the weight of the lifted legs, not by pulling with the arms.
Where they get stuck
Clawing downwards with the hands. Teach them to lift the legs clear of the water.
Stage 35 skills

Breaststroke Kick 10 yd

Pass standard
Kicks 10 yd on a board: heels to seat, feet turned out, whip together.
How to teach it
The whip kick is the hardest movement in the four strokes to teach. Break it into four counts — draw up, turn out, drive, together — and go slowly.
Where they get stuck
Feet never turning out, so it becomes a scissor kick. Hold their feet and walk them through it once.

Breaststroke Kick 25 yd

Pass standard
Kicks 25 yd breaststroke with a glide after every kick, no scissor kick.
How to teach it
There is a glide after every drive, and that glide is the whole of breaststroke efficiency.
Where they get stuck
Kicking continuously with no glide. Ask them to count one after the drive before drawing up.

Dolphin Kick 25 yd

Pass standard
Dolphin kicks 25 yd on the front with a board, wave from the chest.
How to teach it
Twenty-five yards of dolphin kick is the physical base for butterfly. The up and the down should be even, not all down.
Where they get stuck
Only kicking down. Keep saying: the way up has to work too.

Breath Holding 15 sec

Pass standard
Holds 15 seconds submerged, calm, and surfaces under control.
How to teach it
Fifteen seconds prepares them for underwater work. It is not a contest. Any discomfort and it stops.
Where they get stuck
Turning it into a contest. Never let swimmers hold their breath against each other.

Reach and Throw Rescue

Pass standard
Reaches with a pole and throws a ring to a person 5 yd away, staying out of the water.
How to teach it
Not going in yourself is the entire point of this skill. Say it over and over: reach first, throw second, never jump.
Where they get stuck
The coach going in to demonstrate the rescue. A wrong demonstration is worse than none.
Level 6

Four Strokes

Butterfly and breaststroke arrive and the four strokes are complete. Two new strokes are being taught at once, which makes this the heaviest level — do not rush it. Endurance and turns take shape here too.

Stage 14 skills

Butterfly 10 yd

Pass standard
Swims 10 yd butterfly, two kicks per arm cycle, chin leading the breath.
How to teach it
Ten yards is enough for butterfly to begin with. The one-pull-two-kicks rhythm matters more than the distance.
Where they get stuck
Stretching the distance until it becomes one pull, one kick. Ten good yards is better.

Breaststroke 15 yd

Pass standard
Swims 15 yd breaststroke in pull-breathe-kick-glide order.
How to teach it
Breaststroke runs pull, breathe, drive, glide. Get the order wrong and no amount of practice makes it flow.
Where they get stuck
Driving before pulling. Call the four counts out loud, one at a time.

Individual Medley Kicking

Pass standard
Kicks 25 yd of each stroke in IM order without a break between them.
How to teach it
Medley kicking drills the order of the four strokes and checks all four sets of legs still work.
Where they get stuck
Stopping for a rest between strokes. Ask for it unbroken.

Treading Water 2 min

Pass standard
Treads water 2 minutes in deep water, last 30 seconds hands out of the water.
How to teach it
Two minutes of treading is the final proof of deep-water safety. Hands out of the water for the last thirty seconds.
Where they get stuck
Sinking lower as it goes on. Switch them to a whip-kick tread to save energy.
Stage 24 skills

Freestyle 200 yd

Pass standard
Swims 200 yd freestyle continuous with even pacing, turns included.
How to teach it
Two hundred yards is where endurance starts. Teach pacing: no more than five seconds between any two 50s.
Where they get stuck
Going out too hard on the first 50. Put a stopwatch on it so they see the gap themselves.

Backstroke 100 yd

Pass standard
Swims 100 yd backstroke continuous, straight, with turns.
How to teach it
A hundred yards of backstroke with turns, where holding a straight line is the real challenge.
Where they get stuck
Drifting out of the lane. Use the ceiling lights as a guide line.

Backstroke Flip Turn

Pass standard
Rolls to the front, somersaults and pushes off on the back, all within the legal one stroke.
How to teach it
The backstroke turn has a lot of rule detail. Once they roll to the front, one arm pull only before the somersault.
Where they get stuck
Two or three extra strokes after rolling over. That is a disqualification.

Standing Dive

Pass standard
Standing dive from the edge: enters through one hole, hands first, no belly-flop.
How to teach it
Check the water depth before any standing dive. Hands in first, and the body follows through the same hole.
Where they get stuck
Driving the feet up too hard, so they over-rotate.
Stage 35 skills

Butterfly 25 yd

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd butterfly without stopping, rhythm holding to the wall.
How to teach it
Twenty-five yards of butterfly asks whether the rhythm survives to the wall. The back half is where it goes.
Where they get stuck
The last five yards turning into a struggle. Slow the rhythm rather than lose the shape.

Breaststroke 25 yd

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd breaststroke with a visible glide every stroke.
How to teach it
Every cycle of a 25 yd breaststroke should have a visible glide in it.
Where they get stuck
An over-large arm pull that swallows the glide.

Underwater Dolphin Kick

Pass standard
Dolphin kicks 15 yd underwater off the wall on one breath.
How to teach it
Fifteen yards of underwater dolphin is a real competitive weapon. Mind the distance the rules allow.
Where they get stuck
Going short of air chasing distance. Any discomfort and it stops immediately.

Two-Hand Touch Turn

Pass standard
Turns at the wall in breaststroke and butterfly with both hands touching at the same time.
How to teach it
Breaststroke and butterfly turn under different rules from freestyle: both hands must touch the wall together. In a race, one hand is an immediate disqualification.
Where they get stuck
One hand reaching first when they are tired. Ask for two hands from the very first lesson, not from L7.

Clothed Swim (25 yd)

Pass standard
Swims 25 yd clothed without goggles, then treads 1 minute still clothed.
How to teach it
Twenty-five yards clothed plus a minute of treading is a complete fall-in survival rehearsal.
Where they get stuck
Clothes that are too light. Use real cotton that soaks up water.
Level 7

Competitive Swimming

From swimming well to not being disqualified. Pacing, starts, the rules of turns, the individual medley. This level has no real finish line — the last stage has six skills side by side and takes a long time. That is normal.

Stage 15 skills

Freestyle 50 yd Timed

Pass standard
Swims 50 yd freestyle on the clock and improves the time over three attempts.
How to teach it
A timed swim is about whether they are improving, not about the number. Take the trend across three attempts.
Where they get stuck
Comparing the time with another swimmer. Compare it only with their own last one.

Freestyle 25 yd Sprint

Pass standard
All-out 25 yd freestyle with no breath in the last 5 yd.
How to teach it
A sprint is won or lost in the last five yards, and those are the ones without a breath.
Where they get stuck
Not breathing at all for the whole swim. That is breath holding, not sprinting.

Freestyle 400 yd

Pass standard
Swims 400 yd freestyle continuous, pace held within 5 seconds per 50.
How to teach it
Four hundred yards is a pacing lesson. Every 50 within five seconds of the others to pass.
Where they get stuck
Fast for the first 200 and falling apart over the second.

Backstroke 200 yd

Pass standard
Swims 200 yd backstroke continuous with legal turns.
How to teach it
Two hundred yards of backstroke with legal turns — fitness and rules tested at the same time.
Where they get stuck
Turns stop being legal once they are tired.

Clothed Swim (50 yd)

Pass standard
Swims 50 yd clothed without goggles, then removes clothing while treading.
How to teach it
Fifty yards clothed, undressing in the water: the final test of every survival skill.
Where they get stuck
Undressing where they can stand.
Stage 25 skills

Breaststroke Underwater Pullout

Pass standard
One pull-down: full pull to the thighs, one dolphin kick, recovery, break out.
How to teach it
The pullout is one long pull and one dolphin kick, in the order the rules allow. Out of order is a disqualification.
Where they get stuck
Two pulls, or the dolphin kick before the pull.

Breaststroke 50 yd

Pass standard
Swims 50 yd breaststroke with a legal turn and a legal finish.
How to teach it
Fifty yards of breaststroke: both hands together on the wall at the turn and at the finish.
Where they get stuck
One hand at the finish.

Butterfly 50 yd

Pass standard
Swims 50 yd butterfly with a legal turn, rhythm intact on the second length.
How to teach it
Fifty yards of butterfly, where the point is holding the rhythm through the second length.
Where they get stuck
The second length collapsing into one pull, one kick.

Freestyle 500 yd

Pass standard
Swims 500 yd freestyle continuous, counting their own laps.
How to teach it
Five hundred yards means counting their own lengths. That is what a race actually asks of them.
Where they get stuck
Losing count. Teach them to mark it every 100 yards.

Racing Start and Backstroke Start

Pass standard
Legal racing start from the block and a legal backstroke start from the wall.
How to teach it
Both the block start and the backstroke start have rule detail. Moving early is a disqualification.
Where they get stuck
Going before the signal. Drill it: nothing moves until you hear it.
Stage 36 skills

Stroke Transitions and Turns

Pass standard
Legal transitions between all four strokes plus every turn in an IM.
How to teach it
Every turn between two strokes has its own rule. Break them out one at a time, then join them up.
Where they get stuck
Practising all four turns jumbled together.

Individual Medley 100 yd

Pass standard
Swims 100 yd IM in stroke order with legal transitions throughout.
How to teach it
The 100 yd IM is where the whole programme comes together. The order is butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke, freestyle.
Where they get stuck
Getting the order wrong. Have them say it back before they step up.

Freestyle (Race Legal)

Pass standard
Freestyle that would not be disqualified: legal turn, legal finish, no walking.
How to teach it
Race-legal freestyle: touch at the turn, touch at the finish, and never stand on the bottom.
Where they get stuck
Standing up mid-turn to reset.

Backstroke (Race Legal)

Pass standard
Backstroke that would not be disqualified: stays on the back, legal turn and finish.
How to teach it
Race-legal backstroke: on the back the whole way, apart from the part of the turn the rules allow.
Where they get stuck
Rolling over at the finish.

Breaststroke (Race Legal)

Pass standard
Breaststroke that would not be disqualified: symmetric, one pull one kick, two-hand touch.
How to teach it
Race-legal breaststroke: symmetric left and right, one pull to one kick, both hands touching together.
Where they get stuck
A scissor kick appearing, or an uneven pull.

Butterfly (Race Legal)

Pass standard
Butterfly that would not be disqualified: simultaneous arms, no flutter kick, two-hand touch.
How to teach it
Race-legal butterfly: both arms recovering together, no flutter kick, both hands touching together.
Where they get stuck
One hand reaching the wall first when they are tired.

One thing still unsolved: stage 3 of Level 7 has six skills side by side, and all six must read 100 before the programme is complete. Swimmers sit there a long time and the bar barely moves. That is deliberate — racing standards are slow by nature — but say so to parents in advance, so they do not think their child has stalled.
